1 · Align by hand

The overlay is the camera this frame had: the rig's seed lens — 582.3 px/rad with its k terms, set in Overlay model below — pointing at the zenith for this time and place, north up, until the pins measure the real one. Rotate, mirror and drag the photo under it: bigger circles are brighter stars, the faint guide rings mark 30° and 60° from the zenith (90° too, if the disc reaches the horizon), the dashed circle is the disc rim and the cross is where the zenith lands. Drag to shift · arrow keys nudge 1 px, shift+arrow 10 px.

Pin a star instead of dragging: click its circle, then the same star in the photo. Every pin refits the whole camera at once, so the lines show the error each pair still carries: one pin places the optical axis, two add the roll, three also measure the tilt of the axis (a frame that does not point at the zenith), and the circles move onto the real stars as soon as they can. Four stars also calibrate the lens, and a fifth, sixth and seventh buy the theta^5, theta^7 and theta^9 terms; further stars tighten it instead of buying more distortion terms. Click a pinned circle again — or the ✕ beside it in the readout — to take that pin out.

Frame view

The photograph is shown as the camera wrote it — full resolution, no tone curve — so what you see is what the pins are measured on. A night frame is nearly black, though: a gamma curve on the picture lifts the shadows for pinning. Nothing is sent anywhere and no pixel of the file changes.
